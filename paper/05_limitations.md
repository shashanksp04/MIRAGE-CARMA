# Limitations and Threats to Validity

## Enduring Scope and Method Limitations

The study is scoped to multimodal agricultural questions represented by MIRAGE [@dongre2025mirage]. The benchmark includes identification, management, and gardening-guidance tasks, but it covers one benchmark distribution, seven task categories, one to three images per record, and the geographic metadata available in its records. It does not establish generality to other agricultural workflows, languages, countries, sensor modalities, or longitudinal decision settings. A generated response is also not equivalent to a diagnosis supported by field sampling, laboratory testing, or repeated observation. The architecture should therefore be understood as a method for evidence-supported response generation within the benchmark setting rather than as a substitute for those processes.

Any model-related conclusions will be conditional on the exact checkpoints and serving configurations assigned to each role. The response generator, evidence controller, query-enrichment model, keyword extractor, and evaluator can share a checkpoint, but need not do so. Differences in role assignment, model revision, reasoning mode, tool-call support, decoding, or numerical precision can affect visual interpretation, evidence acquisition, and measurement. Consequently, evidence from a finalized configuration will not by itself generalize to the Qwen-3 family as a whole or to multimodal language models generally. The same restriction applies to the architecture: the study evaluates dense vector retrieval, exact-match metadata scopes, a fixed retrieval-confidence heuristic, and prompt-directed tool interfaces. Its conclusions do not constitute universal estimates of the value of metadata, adaptive retrieval, or online evidence acquisition under other retrieval and control designs.

## Corpus Selection and Metadata Proxies

Retrieval quality is bounded by the coverage, accuracy, and temporal currency of the curated base collection and runtime collection. Relevant material absent from both stores cannot be recovered by the retriever, while missing or inaccurate payload fields can change which metadata scope is selected. The curated corpus also depends on an LLM qualification gate: fixed-length segments are classified into agricultural categories, merged at the document level, and excluded when the merged label is `msc`. No validated human audit or measured error profile for this selection stage is established in the repository. This does not show that the gate selected documents incorrectly, but it means that corpus coverage and topical composition inherit the qualification model's decision boundary and the fixed acceptance rule.

Geographic retrieval uses USDA plant-hardiness zones, which summarize average annual extreme minimum winter temperature [@usda2023phzm]. County-state pairs are mapped to a zone; state-only inputs and unmatched counties use a state-modal fallback. A hardiness zone is a coarse climate proxy and does not encode local pest pressure, precipitation, soil, elevation, cultivar, regulatory jurisdiction, or extension-service boundaries. Locations in the same zone can therefore differ along variables relevant to agricultural guidance, while the state-modal fallback suppresses within-state variation. The metadata and web-domain policies are also centered on U.S. state, land-grant, and university mappings, limiting direct transfer to regions organized around different administrative and advisory systems.

Temporal filtering combines heterogeneous date meanings. The `month_year` payload may represent a source date, a provider-reported page age or month-year, or the acquisition month when those provider fields do not yield a usable value. Exact matching therefore need not compare equivalent notions of publication time. Optional query enrichment has a related dependency on a state-indexed crop dictionary and an enrichment model following an allowlist instruction. Its character-subsequence validator blocks deletion, substitution, and reordering of the original question, but does not independently establish token-level allowlist compliance or the agronomic appropriateness of an insertion. Dictionary coverage, source quality, and state granularity consequently bound the validity of this feature.

## Confidence and Controller Construct Validity

The confidence evaluation is a fixed routing heuristic over mean cosine similarity, hit count, score consistency, and one diagnostic metadata-scope label. The weights and thresholds have no recorded calibration procedure. Hit count is not coverage of all semantic aspects of a question, score consistency is not factual agreement among sources, and the diagnostic scope can be inherited from only one collection after evidence from both collections has been merged. The score is therefore a control signal for this pipeline rather than a calibrated probability that the evidence is sufficient or correct.

Confidence evaluation also performs a second retrieval instead of scoring the passages returned by the initial retrieval call. Its model-supplied query, title, `month_year`, and retrieval budget can differ from the initial call, and the shared runtime collection can change between the two calls. The resulting confidence label therefore characterizes the second retrieved set, which need not be identical to the evidence first shown to the controller. This limits interpretations that treat confidence as a stable assessment of a single evidence set.

The evidence policy is prompt-directed. Python determines which tools are available, while the language-model controller chooses their arguments and is instructed, rather than deterministically forced, to invoke them in the intended order. Tool availability, tool invocation, and correct ordered compliance are thus distinct treatments. Model-supplied arguments can additionally override the run-level progressive-metadata and domain-filter settings in some calls. Component-level attribution consequently requires both a contrast that isolates the component and manipulation checks showing the effective calls, arguments, and evidence exposure. Several repository-supported candidate conditions instead change bundles of mechanisms and can support only bundle-level interpretations.

The persisted JSONL records terminal RAG status, retry attempt, endpoint, context use under the driver's success heuristic, and observed web-search use, but it does not retain enough structured information to reconstruct the evidence path or verify ordered treatment compliance. In particular, selected strategies, confidence values, retrieved chunk identities, effective enriched queries, and ingestion outcomes are absent. Unless an external trace was retained, this logging boundary limits post hoc analysis to capability access and coarse terminal behavior.

## Online Acquisition and Shared-State Reproducibility

Live web augmentation varies with provider ranking, page availability and revision, network behavior, and acquisition time. A versioned cache of provider responses can support replay of the search stage; complete live-search artifacts can document a run but cannot guarantee recreation after pages or rankings change. The domain-filter contrast has an additional construct limitation: enabling location-aware filtering changes the query string by adding site restrictions and suffix exclusions. It therefore bundles a source-domain restriction with a query-formulation change, even when the provider response is otherwise frozen. Any effect of that contrast cannot be attributed solely to the authority or geographic relevance of the returned domains.

All workers in a run share one mutable runtime collection. Evidence acquired for one record can be retrieved for another, and the state visible to a request depends on dispatch order, completion order, retries, and the interleaving of searches and upserts. Identical benchmark order alone does not reproduce this exposure under concurrent execution. If the finalized protocol retains accumulating runtime memory, analysis must treat observations as clustered within independently initialized runs, preserve order and ingestion traces, and use multiple fresh runs to estimate run-level variation; a single stateful run cannot provide uncertainty estimates across run units. Per-record collection isolation or a frozen evidence replay would support item-level independence more directly, but would evaluate a different memory policy.

## Benchmark and Evaluation Validity

MIRAGE provides a finite sample of agricultural questions and a single expert response for each record. A reference-based rubric can therefore reward agreement with the supplied answer without directly measuring agronomic outcomes, and it may not represent the full set of acceptable recommendations or phrasings. Model-based judging adds dependence on the judge checkpoint, prompt, decoding behavior, schema validation, and repeat stability. Human validation is not required to define these metrics, but without an agreement or spot-check study their alignment with expert assessment remains externally uncalibrated.

The identification and management evaluations also measure different constructs. Identification combines a binary entity score with a 0--4 reasoning score, whereas management uses four 0--4 dimensions and a weighted composite. These quantities should be reported separately; their scales do not justify a direct pooled comparison or a single overall claim without an additional, prespecified aggregation rationale.

The checked-in benchmark contains 8,188 rows and 8,184 unique identifier strings. Four identifiers each occur on two rows with distinct image files. When the full file is passed through the current ID-keyed splitter, one member of each pair is overwritten; resume logic can also treat a recorded identifier as covering the other member. These are properties of the code path, not evidence that an executed cohort lost those rows. A collision-free row identifier and a frozen common cohort remove this ambiguity for new runs. If legacy outputs are used, the affected rows and condition-specific completion sets must instead be audited before paired analysis.

## Protocol-Dependent Threats Requiring Resolution

Several prerequisites for interpreting experimental results are not established by the repository artifacts and are therefore treated here as unresolved protocol facts rather than as observed limitations. First, the checked-in evidence does not establish a Qwen-3 subject-model run or the final judge. Second, the repository defines seven named RAG configurations plus a separate direct-generation path, rather than an authoritative nine-condition matrix. Its agent configuration applies five tool-level switches but does not enact the declared curated-base and crop-dictionary fields; those factors are controlled separately by driver arguments. Condition labels alone therefore do not establish treatment assignment. After verification, the residual controller limitation is that enabled capabilities measure access rather than guaranteed per-record invocation.

Third, interpretation of retrieval conditions depends on the actual curated snapshot, source inventory, qualification audit, embedding revisions, and crop-dictionary artifact. [DETAIL REQUIRES VERIFICATION: base snapshot and hash, completed build and source manifest, collection promotion procedure, document and chunk counts, embedding-path equivalence, and crop-dictionary path and hash.] The final judge checkpoint, rubric prompt, judgment repetition, invalid-output policy, any human validation, and uncertainty analysis also remain [DETAIL REQUIRES VERIFICATION]. The Experimental Setup should report these executed identities and procedures; the durable limitations are corpus-selection dependence and judge-mediated construct validity.

Finally, the executed cohort and shared-state analysis are unresolved. ## Pre-Execution Implementation Risks

The implementation audit identified correctable code-path risks: the canonical no-evidence message satisfies the driver's length-based success rule; the checked-in launcher does not pass its ablation identifier and supplies a base-collection value parsed as false; ID-keyed cohort handling conflates duplicate identifiers; and the local-judge path does not fully enforce declared score types and ranges before unsuccessful judgments can be removed during cleanup. These findings do not establish that any completed experimental run used the affected paths or that they changed an outcome. They should be corrected before new runs. If existing outputs are retained, run artifacts and traces must determine the exact affected records and conditions; only verified deviations should then be reported as threats to the executed study.
